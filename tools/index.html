The design industry and the tools we use have been evolving constantly. 7 years ago I helped my team transition from Sketch to Figma, an emerging competitor to Sketch and Adobe products. We had to rethink how we worked, because the plugins and accelerators we used day-to-day didn't translate one to one. We were thrilled to leverage Figma components and auto-layout to spend less time maintaining files and more time designing. The transition turned out to be a great opportunity to refresh our processes and unlock faster design development then we had previously with the old software.

As my team lead put it, most product teams have a whole group dedicated to growth, people whose job is managing the conversion funnel and improving the experience. We didn't have that, and leadership was paying more and more attention to our numbers. I took the gap as a challenge: what could I actually make possible with Claude?

So I built a small growth team out of AI agents on Claude Code. The heart of it is a knowledge base I maintain, how our funnel is defined, what every event means, how the product really works, and the agents work from that shared context. Instead of a generic chatbot guessing, I (and now the team) can ask a real question about our product and get a fast, grounded answer. I have no formal SQL background, but with this I pull insights from our data that PostHog's own interface can't touch.

Every morning it checks each step of the funnel against its own history and tracks down anything that drifts. It's become our early-warning system: it's how I caught a payment step that had quietly ballooned to a 22-second load, and a checkout bug silently dropping a chunk of our orders, both within hours instead of at the next weekly review. When I need a view PostHog can't give me, or want to share a result, it publishes to a live forecasting site that's now the team's single source of truth for the funnel. A few teammates, including my manager, point their own Claude at the same setup. It's been great helping level up the team's AI usage. As one teammate put it, "Crushing it w Claude, makes work fun."

I really enjoy being able to build my online portfolio myself. In the past, I'll admit that my limited skills prevented me from executing on every idea that crossed my mind. With recent updates to my portfolio, it's been awesome having Cursor to help execute on my ideas. I also noticed how having an agent to work with changed my development flow from a linear figma-to-code pipeline to instead be a more fluid back and forth working in parallel to arrive at the final product. It's been fun to have a random thought driving home or get some specific feedback from a friend and be able to pretty quickly try it out, testing the idea out live. This has helped me push through roadblocks and iterate consistently to build out this space.
